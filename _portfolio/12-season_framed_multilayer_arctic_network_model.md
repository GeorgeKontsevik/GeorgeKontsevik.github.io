An **Environment-Framed Network (EFN)** model for studying how seasonal transport conditions change access to services in Arctic settlements.

### Model

The transport backbone combines regular roads, winter roads, water transport and aviation. Temperature determines which links are available, while travel-time limits constrain access to service providers. Network layers represent population flows for individual services. As transport conditions change, the model recalculates feasible connections and redistributes demand between providers.

### Findings

Across four Arctic regional settlement systems, up to **40% of settlements** change service providers, while **15–20% become temporarily isolated for 2–4 months** during seasonal transitions. The study examines both transport connectivity and the stability of service catchments.
